01 · UI/UX Arena

3 members

Create a useful, clear and visually strong digital experience from a problem statement revealed at the venue.

Challenge format

  • Understand the user problem and define a focused solution.
  • Prepare key screens, a navigable prototype or a clearly connected flow.
  • Present the design choices, target user and expected impact.
  • Use a design tool available on your own laptop.

Judging focus

  • Problem understanding and originality.
  • Usability, information flow and accessibility.
  • Visual consistency and completeness.
  • Clarity of the final presentation.
Bring: charged laptop, charger and your preferred design software. Pre-built final submissions are not allowed; the competition work must be produced during the event.
Register for UI/UX Arena

02 · Code Sprint

3 members

Success depends on how well the team thinks, connects clues and applies programming concepts, not only on writing lengthy programs.

Challenge format

  • A mix of logic, patterns, programming concepts and code-based puzzles.
  • Tasks may include output prediction, code sequencing, reasoning challenges and short implementations.
  • Challenges begin with accessible concepts and become progressively tougher.
  • Teams must combine logical thinking with programming knowledge to advance.

Scoring focus

  • Correctness of solutions.
  • Logical reasoning and problem-solving.
  • Successful completion of challenges.
  • Hint usage may affect the score.
  • Completion time is mainly considered for tie-breaking.
No laptop required: venue systems and the required coding setup will be provided by the organizers. Bring your college ID and report on time.
Register for Code Sprint

03 · Bug Hunt

3 members

Success depends on how carefully the team reads the code, spots patterns, connects clues and traces what went wrong before choosing the right fix.

Challenge format

  • A progressive mix of bug-spotting puzzles, output tracing and code correction challenges.
  • Tasks may include incorrect conditions, loops, logic, unexpected outputs and short code fixes.
  • Early challenges focus on easier-to-detect issues, while later stages introduce hidden failures and connected bugs.
  • Teams must investigate the cause, apply the right correction and verify the result.

Scoring focus

  • Correct identification of bugs.
  • Accuracy of the applied fix.
  • Understanding of why the error occurs.
  • Hint usage may affect the score.
  • Completion time is mainly considered for tie-breaking.
No laptop required: venue systems and the required debugging setup will be provided by the organizers. Bring your college ID and report on time.
Register for Bug Hunt

04 · Checkmate

Individual

A focused chess competition for players who can calculate accurately, manage time and stay composed.

Match format

  • Pairing structure depends on the final number of participants.
  • Time control and round instructions are announced before play begins.
  • Standard touch-move and fair-play expectations apply.
  • Players must report the result to the assigned coordinator.

Player rules

  • Phones and outside assistance are not allowed during play.
  • Respect the clock, board and opponent.
  • Do not disturb games in progress.
  • The arbiter’s decision is final for disputes.
Bring: college ID and arrive before the reporting time. Chess boards and clocks will be managed according to venue availability.
Register for Checkmate
